Research

My research focuses on space systems — from the OSPREY 6U CubeSat mission concept and microwave-assisted lunar recycling to control systems for a 6-DOF robotic arm.

High Power Microwave Systems for Lunar Missions

At the Space Propulsion and Environments Laboratory, I researched high power microwave (HPM) systems applications for future lunar missions. I engineered a technical design and proposal for a lunar recycling system powered by a centralized microwave network.

  • High power microwave systems applications for future lunar missions
  • Technical design and proposal for a lunar recycling system powered by a centralized microwave network
  • ISRU and catalyst integration to recycle plastics into rocket fuel precursors

NASA LunaRecycle Challenge

As Research Lead (Phase 1) and Chief Engineer (Phase 2), I directed research and engineering efforts for Penn State's LunaRecycle team competing in NASA's challenge to recycle waste on the lunar surface. After Phase 1 funding, I was appointed Laboratory Lead at SSPL in August 2025.

  • Milestone Round win and two Innovation Awards totaling $125,000 ($50,000 Milestone plus $75,000 for Prototype and Digital Twin Innovation Awards)
  • MATLAB heat transfer model improving energy efficiency by ~15%
  • Microwave-assisted plastic pyrolysis proof-of-concept fabricated for under $500
  • RECLAIM processes metal and plastic feedstock in two independent chambers using high-power microwaves
  • SysML requirements linked to a Digital Twin with live pressure and temperature telemetry
  • Chief Engineer for the Milestone Round and for half of the Final Round before the SDL internship
  • Integrated and tested RECLAIM under vacuum conditions and temperatures exceeding 500°C

OSPREY 6U CubeSat Mission Concept

As a NASA–AFRL Systems Engineering Intern (UNP MC-4) at the Space Dynamics Laboratory, I developed a mission concept for OSPREY, a 6U CubeSat to identify and geolocate ocean plastics in the Pacific Ocean using onboard multispectral imaging and mission operations analysis.

  • ADCS trade studies converting pointing and attitude-knowledge requirements into reaction-wheel targets and star-tracker sensitivity selections
  • STK orbital design for imaging-pass timing, ground-track coverage, and S-band downlink within power and ground-contact constraints
  • Python pipeline compressing Landsat multispectral imagery into spectral indices and scoring plastic signatures for detection feasibility
  • Consultation with NASA and AFRL engineers and systems engineering through PDR

6-DOF Robotic Arm Control Systems

As a Control Systems Researcher in the Astrodynamics Group of Penn State, I modeled a 6-DOF robotic arm in Python using DH parameters and NumPy, and simulated the system within Manim.

  • Forward and inverse kinematics, Monte Carlo methods, and a Gaussian mixture model
  • Python, NumPy, and Manim simulation of the 6-DOF arm
